# Dry run: 5,040 trials

**Run `dry-01`, 2026-09-10.** 42 tasks × 12 configurations × 10 repetitions,
48 workers, rate limiter aiming at 25 requests/second.

Run to check the platform holds at a hundredth of the intended scale before
committing to 300,000. It did, and it produced a real result on the way.

## What it cost

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Trials | 5,040 |
| Model time | 12,956 seconds |
| Mean trial | 2.57 s |
| Tokens | 15.4M in, 1.5M out |
| Cost | **$0.75** |
| Infrastructure errors | **0** |
| Ran out of budget | 5 |

At this cost, 300,000 trials is roughly **$45**.

## The result

Pass rate over scored trials, so exhausted trials are excluded rather than
counted as wrong.

| | terse | careful | plan-first | model |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **gpt-oss-20b** | 98.6% | 97.4% | 98.1% | **98.0%** |
| **nova-micro** | 84.5% | 91.7% | 89.5% | **88.6%** |
| **nova-lite** | 82.1% | 86.2% | 86.9% | **85.1%** |
| **ministral-3b** | **37.1%** | 80.9% | 84.0% | **67.4%** |
| **prompt** | **75.6%** | **89.0%** | **89.6%** | |

### The prompt matters about as much as the model

Best model to worst is 98.0% → 67.4%, a spread of 31 points. Best prompt to
worst is 89.6% → 75.6%, a spread of 14. Choosing the wording is roughly half as
consequential as choosing the model, and considerably cheaper.

### But only for weak models

The interesting number is not either average -- it is that they interact, which
is the thing a one-dimensional comparison cannot see at all.

```
prompt cost, terse vs the better of the other two, by model

gpt-oss-20b     +0.5   terse is fractionally the best
nova-lite       -4.8
nova-micro      -7.2
ministral-3b   -46.9
```

`gpt-oss-20b` does not need to be told to be careful; it already is. Ministral
needs it badly enough that the terse prompt costs it 47 points, taking it from
usable to unusable.

That has a practical reading: **prompt engineering is worth most where the model
is weakest**, and a team that tunes prompts against a strong model will conclude
prompts do not matter much.

### Flakiness tracks weakness

Tasks passed sometimes and failed other times, out of 42:

| | terse | careful | plan-first |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| gpt-oss-20b | 6 | 9 | 8 |
| nova-micro | 23 | 12 | 15 |
| nova-lite | 24 | 13 | 12 |
| ministral-3b | 37 | 34 | 25 |

Ministral on the terse prompt is flaky on 37 of 42 tasks -- nearly everything it
touches is a coin flip. A single trial per task would have reported some number
between 0% and 100% for it and given no hint that the number was noise.

### What actually goes wrong

| Count | Reason |
| --- | --- |
| 335 | missing hotel booking -- booked the wrong hotel, or none |
| 109 | unwanted hotel booking -- booked extra rooms |
| 48 | missing flight booking |

The two shapes are different failures. "Missing" is choosing wrongly. "Unwanted"
is doing more than was asked, which costs the traveller money and is the failure
the `careful` prompt is written to prevent.

## What this says about the platform

- **Zero infrastructure errors in 5,040 trials.** The limiter held at 25/s and
  nothing was throttled to the point of being abandoned. The earlier fix for
  malformed tool names is holding too.
- **Five trials ran out of budget**, all on ministral -- 0.1%, and recorded as
  exhausted rather than failed, which is the distinction that keeps a weak
  model's loops out of its pass rate.
- **The grader fix mattered enormously.** Before it, 85% of failures were the
  grader's case sensitivity. None of the numbers above would have meant anything.

## What was not measured, and why

**Wall-clock throughput.** The run's own summary line was lost to a scrolled
terminal, and results carry no timestamp, so the sustained trials-per-second
cannot be recovered after the fact. That is the one number the dry run existed
to produce.

Trials now record when they finished, so the next run can measure it. Until
then the 300,000-trial estimate rests on the arithmetic -- roughly four model
calls per trial at 25 calls per second is about 13 hours -- rather than on
anything observed.
